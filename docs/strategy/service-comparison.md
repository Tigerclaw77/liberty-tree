# Liberty Tree Service Opportunity Comparison

Date: 2026-07-07  
Status: Internal investment committee memo  
Decision context: One founder, limited capital, one expert reviewer, AI improving over the next three years

## Executive Recommendation

If Liberty Tree can pursue only one service for the next 12 months, it should pursue **PFAS Evidence Packets**, narrowly positioned as source-indexed evidence discovery and documentation readiness for manufacturers and suppliers responding to specific buyer, retailer, regulatory, or internal PFAS documentation requests.

This is not because PFAS is the easiest service. It is not. FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps have a stronger near-term regulatory event and a cleaner clause-mapping structure. FSVP Evidence Binders have a stable regulatory base and a broad importer market.

PFAS wins because it is the only candidate where Liberty Tree has uncovered a potentially distinctive operating advantage: finding material evidence that ordinary search, naive AI, and generic document review may miss. If Liberty Tree's emerging evidence-discovery methodology proves repeatable, the service can become more than report writing. It can become a cross-domain evidence discovery business.

The recommendation is conditional. If Liberty Tree cannot convert PFAS discovery rigor into paid buyer confidence within the next several pilots, QMSR should be reconsidered as the more standardized revenue wedge, but only if Liberty Tree has access to a credible medical-device QMS reviewer.

## Scoring Method

Scores are 1 to 10, where 10 is most attractive for Liberty Tree.

Two criteria require interpretation:

- Human review required: 10 means the review burden is light or operationally manageable; 1 means expert review is a severe delivery bottleneck.
- Risk of commoditization: 10 means low commoditization risk; 1 means high commoditization risk.

## Service Candidates

### PFAS Evidence Packets

PFAS Evidence Packets organize public and client-provided product, supplier, SDS, declaration, testing, customer-request, and regulatory-reference evidence into a source-indexed packet. The service does not claim compliance, provide a legal opinion, or certify product status.

Strategic character: messy, high-discovery, high-trust, defensibility-driven.

### FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps would compare a medical device manufacturer's current quality system documents and records against FDA's revised Quality Management System Regulation and incorporated ISO 13485:2016 structure. The service would identify missing, weak, obsolete, or unmapped documents. It would not perform full QMS implementation, audit certification, or legal representation.

Strategic character: standardized, urgent, expert-heavy, competitive.

### FSVP Evidence Binders

FSVP Evidence Binders would help food importers organize supplier verification evidence, hazard-related documentation, supplier approvals, audits, corrective actions, written assurances, translations, and records needed to support Foreign Supplier Verification Program readiness. The service would not act as the importer's legal counsel or replace a qualified individual where required.

Strategic character: stable, broad, documentation-heavy, more commoditized.

## Detailed Score Justifications

### 1. Market Demand

PFAS Evidence Packets: **8/10**

Public evidence shows real documentation demand across state PFAS product laws, EPA TSCA reporting pressure, retailer and OEM supplier requirements, textile and apparel restricted substance programs, food-contact packaging scrutiny, and customer declaration workflows. The demand is broad but uneven. The central uncertainty is not whether PFAS documentation requests exist; they do. The uncertainty is whether buyers will pay boutique fixed-price fees for evidence organization that is not legal advice, testing, or certification.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

Demand is real because FDA's revised QMSR is now effective and affects finished medical device manufacturers. FDA states that QMSR became effective on February 2, 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference into the device CGMP framework. That creates a clear market for document gap assessments. The constraint is that many buyers already have established QMS consultants, notified body relationships, ISO 13485 advisors, and former FDA inspection experts.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **7/10**

Demand exists because FSVP applies broadly to importers of food imported or offered for import into the United States, with records, supplier verification, hazard analysis, corrective action, and availability obligations. The market is large, but buyer willingness may be weaker than PFAS or QMSR because many importers are small, price-sensitive, or already buying low-cost FSVP training, agent, or food safety consultant services.

### 2. Buyer Urgency

PFAS Evidence Packets: **8/10**

Urgency is high when a customer request, retailer portal, state product restriction, TSCA reporting deadline, or sales-blocking compliance questionnaire appears. Urgency is weaker when the buyer is only doing general readiness work. PFAS has strong event-driven pain, but the event varies by sector and jurisdiction.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **9/10**

Urgency is the strongest of the three candidates. QMSR is already in effect as of February 2, 2026, and FDA has moved to the updated device inspection process rather than QSIT. Medical device firms can no longer treat QMSR as a future transition topic. This is a live inspection readiness problem.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **6/10**

Urgency is real when an importer receives an FDA inspection request, import disruption, supplier issue, corrective action trigger, or customer audit. However, FSVP is not a new transition event in 2026. Many buyers have lived with the obligation for years, which lowers the urgency signal unless an immediate inspection or shipment problem exists.

### 3. AI Leverage

PFAS Evidence Packets: **9/10**

AI can materially improve source discovery, product/SKU variant search, hidden-link detection, SDS extraction, declaration comparison, source indexing, evidence matrix drafting, gap detection, and conflict review. The key leverage is not just summarization. It is recall: finding documents that ordinary search misses.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

AI is well suited to clause mapping, document inventory, SOP comparison, record classification, gap extraction, and draft remediation language. The work is highly structured, which helps AI. The downside is that the same structure makes the service more likely to be commoditized as AI tools improve.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **8/10**

AI can organize supplier records, extract hazard references, classify audits and written assurances, track dates, compare supplier files, translate or summarize foreign-language records, and identify missing binder elements. Expert review remains necessary for food safety judgment and qualified-individual responsibilities.

### 4. Human Review Required

PFAS Evidence Packets: **6/10**

Human review is significant but manageable if the service remains evidence-focused. The expert must review chemical terminology, product scope, declaration wording, SDS limitations, regulatory placeholders, and overclaiming risk. The burden rises sharply if the buyer expects legal conclusions, test interpretation, or certification.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **5/10**

Human review is a major bottleneck. A credible reviewer must understand FDA device quality systems, ISO 13485:2016, design controls, CAPA, complaint handling, supplier controls, records, management review, internal audits, labeling, and inspection dynamics. AI can map documents, but a weak reviewer would be obvious to a device QA buyer.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **5/10**

Human review is also a major constraint. FSVP work can require a qualified individual, food safety hazard analysis, supplier performance review, audit interpretation, preventive controls context, and corrective action judgment. The binder can be organized by AI, but the underlying food safety evaluation cannot be hand-waved.

### 5. Regulatory Stability

PFAS Evidence Packets: **4/10**

PFAS is the least stable regulatory environment. Federal reporting rules, state product restrictions, definitions, thresholds, timelines, exemptions, and enforcement expectations are moving quickly. This instability creates demand, but it also increases the risk of outdated packet language and jurisdictional overreach.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

QMSR is comparatively stable. FDA issued the final rule, incorporated ISO 13485:2016, and moved to the new framework effective February 2, 2026. Future ISO or FDA changes can occur, but the central transition is now defined.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **9/10**

FSVP is the most stable of the three. The core FSVP regulation has been in place for years, and the eCFR shows no changes to Subpart L after January 3, 2017. Stability improves repeatability, training, and pricing.

### 6. Repeatability

PFAS Evidence Packets: **7/10**

The packet architecture is repeatable, but discovery varies widely by product, source, industry, customer request, supplier responsiveness, and jurisdiction. Repeatability improves if Liberty Tree narrows to a vertical such as textiles, private-label imports, food-contact packaging, or electronics suppliers.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **9/10**

QMSR is the most repeatable candidate. The service can be anchored to a consistent requirement map, document inventory, quality system process areas, and gap scoring method. Device type and company maturity vary, but the structure is highly reusable.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **8/10**

FSVP binders are repeatable because the regulation creates common record and verification categories. Product hazards, supplier risk, exemptions, and importer roles vary, but the binder logic is consistent enough for repeatable delivery.

### 7. Fixed-Price Suitability

PFAS Evidence Packets: **6/10**

Fixed pricing can work only with strict boundaries: number of products, number of suppliers, public versus client-provided evidence, no supplier-chasing beyond a defined window, and no legal or testing conclusions. Discovery uncertainty is the main margin risk.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

QMSR gap maps are well suited to fixed price if the scope is limited to document review, clause mapping, and gap identification. Implementation, CAPA remediation, audit defense, and procedure rewriting must remain out of scope or priced separately.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **7/10**

FSVP binders can be fixed-price for a defined number of foods, suppliers, and records. The risk is that hazard analysis, supplier nonresponse, audit issues, or corrective action review can pull the engagement into expert-heavy consulting.

### 8. Gross Margin Potential

PFAS Evidence Packets: **8/10**

Gross margin can be attractive because AI can reduce extraction, indexing, matrix drafting, and QA preparation time. The service can plausibly command $7.5k to $20k+ when tied to urgent customer requests or regulatory readiness. The margin risk is uncontrolled discovery and expert review creep.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

QMSR can support strong pricing because medical device quality buyers understand audit readiness and regulatory risk. A few $15k to $30k document gap maps per month could reach meaningful revenue. Expert review cost is higher, but buyers may tolerate higher fees.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **6/10**

FSVP margins may be lower because many importers are price-sensitive and the market has training courses, templates, FSVP agents, and food safety consultants. Higher margin is possible for complex importers or dietary supplement/ingredient companies, but the average buyer may push pricing down.

### 9. Time To First Customer

PFAS Evidence Packets: **6/10**

PFAS requires buyer education because the packet is not legal advice, certification, or testing. The first customer is likely to arrive through a concrete customer request, a lab/law firm referral, or a sector-specific outreach wedge rather than general marketing.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **5/10**

QMSR has a clear trigger, but medical device buyers are conservative. Without visible device QMS credentials, prior FDA inspection experience, or a strong reviewer profile, the first sale may be slow. Trust matters more than the deliverable concept.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **7/10**

FSVP may have the shortest path to a first small customer because the buyer universe is broad and the problem is operational. The downside is that the easiest first customers may be smaller and lower-budget.

### 10. Founder Fit

PFAS Evidence Packets: **8/10**

PFAS has the strongest founder fit because Liberty Tree has already built the packet architecture, sample packet, QA review, improvement roadmap, production workflow, commercial validation, market validation, production simulation, and evidence discovery methodology. The project has developed an authentic point of view.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **5/10**

QMSR is attractive but less founder-native unless the founder has device QA buyer access or can credibly sell around a named expert reviewer. The service risks becoming "another QMS consultant" unless Liberty Tree can package it with distinctive speed and evidence discipline.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **6/10**

FSVP fits the evidence-binder concept, but it does not currently appear to have the same strategic spark as PFAS or the same event timing as QMSR. Founder fit improves if the founder has food importer relationships or FDA food contacts.

### 11. Expert Fit (Chemistry/FDA Background)

PFAS Evidence Packets: **8/10**

Chemistry expertise is directly relevant to PFAS terminology, SDS interpretation, material claims, testing limitations, and source-quality review. FDA background is especially useful in food-contact, packaging, cosmetics, or regulated product contexts, though PFAS also requires state-law and product stewardship awareness.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **7/10**

FDA background is useful, but the ideal reviewer is specifically a medical device QMS, ISO 13485, inspection, CAPA, design control, and supplier quality expert. Chemistry is less central except for certain device materials or manufacturing processes.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **8/10**

FDA food experience is highly relevant, and chemistry can help with hazard, contaminant, ingredient, and supplier documentation questions. The reviewer still needs specific FSVP and food safety competence, not just general FDA familiarity.

### 12. Long-Term Defensibility

PFAS Evidence Packets: **8/10**

PFAS has the best long-term defensibility if Liberty Tree turns evidence discovery into a measured operating capability: source-path knowledge, supplier document patterns, revision tracking, evidence graphs, hidden-link discovery, and documented cases where naive search missed material evidence. This can generalize beyond PFAS.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **6/10**

QMSR defensibility is weaker because the core work is clause mapping and expert review in a mature consulting category. Liberty Tree can compete on speed and packaging, but experienced QMS consultants and AI-enabled compliance tools can imitate much of the workflow.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **5/10**

FSVP defensibility is limited. Binders, checklists, templates, training courses, and consultant services are already familiar. The moat would need to come from a specialized importer vertical, supplier network, or recurring managed service rather than the binder itself.

### 13. Expansion Potential

PFAS Evidence Packets: **9/10**

PFAS naturally expands into other regulated substance evidence packets, supplier evidence discovery, retailer portal response support, product stewardship files, declaration management, test-report indexing, and due-diligence evidence rooms. The underlying capability can move from PFAS to Prop 65, REACH/SVHC, RoHS, TSCA reporting, food-contact documentation, and restricted substance programs.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

QMSR can expand into SOP remediation, CAPA improvement, audit readiness, internal audit preparation, management review support, supplier quality gap maps, design history file reviews, and MDSAP-adjacent readiness. These are commercially attractive but expert-intensive.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **7/10**

FSVP can expand into annual binder maintenance, supplier onboarding, foreign supplier approval files, corrective action registers, import record readiness, dietary supplement ingredient files, and audit support. Expansion is real, but pricing may remain constrained by the food importer market.

### 14. Risk Of Commoditization

PFAS Evidence Packets: **7/10**

The final report format can be commoditized. The defensibility comes from evidence discovery, source indexing, hidden document recovery, and expert confidence ratings. If Liberty Tree only writes polished packets, commoditization risk is high. If it proves superior discovery, risk is lower.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **5/10**

QMSR mapping is vulnerable to commoditization because the requirements are structured and consultants already know how to map procedures to clauses. AI will make first-pass gap maps easier. Expert credibility remains valuable, but the deliverable itself is not hard to imitate.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **4/10**

FSVP is the most commoditized candidate. Templates, binders, training providers, agents, and food safety consultants already exist. AI will make binder assembly even cheaper. Differentiation would require deep supplier operations, importer vertical specialization, or recurring support.

### 15. Ability To Reach $50k/Month

PFAS Evidence Packets: **7/10**

PFAS can reach $50k/month with 3 to 5 standard engagements per month, 2 larger engagements plus support, or a mix of packets and recurring evidence maintenance. The challenge is sales conversion and scope control. The revenue path is plausible but not yet proven.

FDA QMSR Document Gap Maps: **8/10**

QMSR has the strongest direct $50k/month arithmetic. Two to four well-priced gap maps could reach the target if Liberty Tree can win trust. The catch is that delivery depends on a credible device QMS expert and the sales cycle may be harder for a new boutique.

FSVP Evidence Binders: **6/10**

FSVP can reach $50k/month, but likely requires more volume, recurring maintenance, or larger importer accounts. Single-binder pricing may be lower, and the service may require more operational follow-up than expected.

## Winner If Liberty Tree Can Pursue Only One Service

The winner is **PFAS Evidence Packets**.

The investment committee rationale is:

1. PFAS has documented demand and urgency, even if buyer willingness remains unproven.
2. Liberty Tree has already developed the most proprietary thinking around PFAS evidence discovery.
3. The evidence-discovery insight creates a plausible moat that QMSR and FSVP do not currently show.
4. AI improvement over the next three years will commoditize ordinary document mapping faster than it commoditizes disciplined evidence discovery, source-path memory, and reviewer-calibrated confidence.
5. PFAS gives Liberty Tree the best expansion path into a broader compliance evidence business.

The strongest argument against this recommendation is that QMSR may be easier to sell at a higher price during the current transition window. That argument is serious. If Liberty Tree had a known medical-device QMS expert, existing device manufacturer relationships, and proof of buyer access, QMSR could be the first revenue wedge. Without those, QMSR risks becoming a credibility contest against established consultants.

## Strongest Adjacent Services From The Winner

The strongest adjacent services that naturally expand from PFAS are:

1. Regulated substance evidence packets for Prop 65, REACH/SVHC, RoHS, TSCA, and customer restricted substance lists.
2. Supplier evidence discovery audits for manufacturers facing customer declarations.
3. Retailer and OEM compliance request response support.
4. PFAS and restricted-substance source index maintenance for annual updates.
5. Test report and declaration reconciliation for products with supplier, lab, and distributor evidence.
6. Food-contact packaging evidence files where PFAS, FDA food-contact, supplier statements, and customer requirements overlap.
7. Product stewardship evidence rooms for counsel, labs, or consultants.
8. Evidence discovery support for law firms and environmental consultants that need organized source files before advice or testing.

These are adjacencies because they use the same core capability: find the evidence, classify confidence, preserve source traceability, identify gaps, and avoid unsupported conclusions.

## Best Long-Term Moat

PFAS creates the best long-term moat, but only if Liberty Tree turns methodology into operating history.

QMSR has a strong near-term market, but the moat is mostly expert reputation. FSVP has stable demand, but the service shape is familiar and vulnerable to template-level competition. PFAS is less stable and harder to scope, but it exposes a deeper problem: important evidence is fragmented, hidden, conflicting, stale, or inaccessible through naive search.

The moat is not "PFAS expertise" alone. The moat is evidence discovery across documentation-heavy compliance domains.

If Liberty Tree can build a repeatable evidence graph, search heuristics, source-path library, hidden-link recovery discipline, and reviewer confidence model, the capability can outlive PFAS as a single regulatory topic.

## Single-Service Or Multiple Offerings

Liberty Tree should remain single-service initially.

Launching multiple offerings would dilute positioning, sales learning, expert review capacity, QA discipline, and methodology development. It would also make the market story confusing: PFAS, QMSR, and FSVP have different buyers, vocabularies, risk profiles, and trust signals.

The best initial positioning is:

> Liberty Tree helps manufacturers and suppliers build source-indexed PFAS evidence packets when a customer, regulator, retailer, or internal team asks for documentation.

The service should be narrow in public positioning and broad only in internal ambition. QMSR and FSVP should remain watchlist opportunities until Liberty Tree has either:

- A validated paid PFAS motion that can support expansion, or
- A specific expert-led buyer channel that makes QMSR or FSVP unusually easy to sell.

## Investment Committee Recommendation

Recommendation: **Proceed with PFAS Evidence Packets as the sole 12-month service focus.**

Do not launch QMSR and FSVP alongside it.

The committee should approve PFAS under these constraints:

- Sell documentation readiness and evidence discovery, not compliance certification.
- Target urgent buyer-triggered use cases, not general education.
- Narrow the first vertical, likely textiles, private-label consumer products, food-contact packaging, or supplier documentation for retailer/OEM requests.
- Track every case where Liberty Tree finds evidence the buyer missed.
- Measure discovery completeness, expert QA burden, and fixed-price margin.
- Reassess after the first 5 paid engagements.

The committee should not approve broad PFAS consulting, legal interpretation, testing strategy, or regulatory certification as the first offer.

Final view:

PFAS is the messiest candidate, but also the most strategically interesting. QMSR is cleaner and closer to a traditional consulting service. FSVP is stable but likely more commoditized. If Liberty Tree wants the fastest standardized consulting wedge, QMSR deserves a serious look. If Liberty Tree wants to build a differentiated boutique business with a durable evidence-discovery advantage, PFAS is the better bet.
