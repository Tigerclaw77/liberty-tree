# Liberty Tree Commercial Validation Assessment

Date: 2026-07-07
Status: Internal investment committee memo
Decision: Proceed with limited validation

## Executive Conclusion

Liberty Tree can plausibly become a $50k+/month boutique compliance evidence business, but the case is not yet proven. The strongest opportunity is not "PFAS compliance" broadly. It is a narrower wedge: helping manufacturers rapidly organize source-indexed PFAS documentation when customers, retailers, regulators, legal counsel, or supply chain partners ask for evidence.

The business is commercially interesting because the pain is fragmented, document-heavy, and cross-functional. It is also dangerous because buyers may expect legal advice, certification, laboratory testing, or definitive regulatory conclusions that Liberty Tree should not provide without appropriate experts. The evidence packet model only works if the offer is positioned as documentation readiness and expert-reviewed evidence organization, not as a compliance guarantee.

A $50k/month path is realistic only if Liberty Tree can reliably sell 3 to 5 scoped engagements per month, or 2 larger engagements plus recurring support, while keeping delivery under roughly 35 to 55 human hours per standard packet. The primary bottleneck is not software. It is buyer trust, expert review capacity, scope control, and access to real client documentation.

Recommendation: proceed with limited validation. Do not scale delivery or build more infrastructure until paid buyers prove the offer, pricing, and trust model.

## 1. Core Hypothesis

Business hypothesis:

Manufacturers and product companies have urgent PFAS documentation problems that are too messy for internal teams, too narrow for large consulting engagements, and too operational for law firms to handle efficiently. Liberty Tree can sell fixed-price, expert-reviewed evidence packets that organize product, supplier, SDS, declaration, testing, and regulatory-reference documentation into a source-indexed file that supports compliance readiness, customer response, and internal decision-making.

For the model to work, AI must reduce the labor required for extraction, classification, matrix drafting, gap spotting, and source indexing. Human experts must remain focused on judgment, QA, scope boundaries, and wording. The buyer must perceive the packet as a business tool worth $7.5k to $20k+, even though it is not certification, testing, or legal advice.

## 2. Buyer Analysis

### Likely Industries

Best initial fit:

- Textiles, apparel, outdoor gear, footwear, uniforms, and treated fabrics.
- Consumer products, housewares, cookware, furniture, juvenile products, and private-label goods.
- Packaging, food-contact materials, coatings, inks, and adhesives.
- Cosmetics, personal care, and formulated consumer goods.
- Industrial components, gaskets, seals, coatings, lubricants, plastics, and specialty chemicals.
- Electronics, electrical equipment, batteries, lighting, and connected devices.
- Automotive, aerospace, and medical device suppliers facing customer compliance flow-downs.
- Importers, distributors, and contract manufacturers with fragmented supplier documentation.

Weak initial fit:

- Companies needing immediate litigation defense.
- Companies with major known contamination or site remediation issues.
- Enterprises already running mature product compliance platforms.
- Very small companies without a specific customer, retailer, or regulatory trigger.

### Buying Triggers

The most likely triggers are urgent and document-specific:

- A customer requests a PFAS declaration, evidence file, or compliance questionnaire.
- A retailer, OEM, distributor, or government buyer adds PFAS documentation requirements.
- A product line is entering a state or jurisdiction with PFAS-related requirements that must be assessed by counsel or compliance staff.
- A supplier cannot provide clear SDS, declaration, formulation, or article-level documentation.
- A company receives inconsistent internal answers across quality, procurement, EHS, and legal.
- A sales opportunity is blocked by missing compliance documentation.
- Counsel asks the business to assemble source materials before legal review.
- A buyer needs to show board, customer, or audit readiness without claiming certification.
- M&A diligence or supplier qualification exposes documentation gaps.

### Budget Owners And Purchase Order Signers

Likely budget owners:

- Quality and product compliance for customer-facing documentation.
- EHS or regulatory affairs for internal readiness.
- Legal for counsel-directed evidence organization.
- Supply chain/procurement for supplier documentation programs.
- Operations when a commercial shipment or customer approval is at risk.

Likely PO signer:

- For $2.5k-$5k discovery: Director or senior manager.
- For $7.5k-$20k packets: VP Quality, VP Regulatory, VP Operations, VP Supply Chain, or General Counsel.
- For $25k+ expanded work or annual support: VP plus CFO/procurement/legal approval, depending on company policy.

Practical buying insight:

The easiest sale is not "be compliant with PFAS." The easier sale is "respond to this customer or internal review with a source-indexed evidence file in 10 business days."

### Competitive Position Summary

Liberty Tree is not a broad environmental consultant, law firm, testing lab, or enterprise product compliance platform. The viable position is a narrow operator:

"We assemble and QA the PFAS evidence file so your experts, counsel, customers, and internal teams can see what is known, what is sourced, what is missing, and what should happen next."

This is a credible wedge if the buyer has documentation chaos. It is weak if the buyer needs legal conclusions, scientific proof, or enterprise-wide compliance infrastructure.

## 4. Pricing

Pricing should be fixed-fee with strict assumptions, documented exclusions, and change-order triggers. Hourly pricing would undermine the productized premise and expose the buyer to the same uncertainty they already dislike.

Cost assumptions:

- Internal operations labor: $60-$100/hour fully loaded.
- Expert reviewer cost or internal opportunity cost: $150-$250/hour.
- AI/tool variable cost: low relative to labor, but workflow setup, document handling, security, and QA are real operating costs.
- Gross margin targets should exclude founder sales time at first, but that time must be modeled before scaling.

## 5. Delivery Economics

The production workflow estimates current standard production at 10-15 business days after core documents are received, or 3-6 weeks including supplier follow-up. Optimized workflow estimates 4-7 business days after document intake, or 2-4 weeks including supplier follow-up. It estimates standard human labor before AI at roughly 91-179 hours and after AI at roughly 29-66 hours, depending on scope and supplier complexity.

### Best, Likely, And Worst Cases

| Scenario | Conditions | Labor | Expert Review | Turnaround | Price Fit | Gross Margin |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Best case | Buyer has organized product list, SDSs, supplier declarations, and a narrow customer request. Few suppliers. No legal conclusion requested. | 20-30 hours | 4-6 hours | 3-5 business days after intake | $7.5k-$12.5k | 65%-75% |
| Likely case | Partial documentation, 10-30 suppliers, inconsistent declarations, some SDS gaps, and need for careful wording. | 32-55 hours | 8-16 hours | 7-15 business days after intake, longer if supplier follow-up is included | $12.5k-$18k | 50%-65% |
| Worst case | Messy product scope, many variants, supplier nonresponse, unclear regulatory jurisdictions, potential testing need, legal review pressure, and buyer expects conclusions. | 70-120 hours | 20-40 hours | 3-6 weeks or longer | $18k-$35k with change orders | 10%-35% if poorly scoped; 40%+ only with change control |

### Economic Implications

The model only works with three controls:

1. Intake discipline: product families, jurisdictions, suppliers, and output use cases must be defined before pricing.
2. Change-order discipline: new product lines, legal interpretation, testing coordination, and supplier follow-up beyond agreed limits must be separately scoped.
3. Expert QA discipline: reviewers should validate source treatment, confidence ratings, language boundaries, and gap framing, not rework the whole packet.

If those controls hold, the unit economics are good. If they fail, fixed-fee engagements become low-margin consulting projects disguised as products.

1. Buyer discovery and expert/channel interviews in parallel.
2. Paid diagnostics before full packet pilots.
3. One standard pilot with strict scope.

## 9. Final Recommendation

Recommendation: Proceed with limited validation.

Do not proceed immediately as a scaled business. The market pain is plausible, but the monetization, trust, and scope-control assumptions are still too exposed. Do not pivot yet. The core wedge is coherent, timely, and aligned with AI-assisted evidence work. Do not abandon. The combination of regulatory pressure, supplier documentation chaos, and customer-facing evidence needs is commercially real enough to test.

The right next move is a 30-45 day validation sprint with hard commercial gates.

### Go Criteria

Proceed toward first commercial launch only if Liberty Tree can show:

- At least 20 buyer conversations across 2 focused verticals.
- At least 3 paid opportunities, including 2 paid diagnostics or 1 standard packet pilot.
- A standard packet price accepted at $10k+ or a diagnostic price accepted at $2.5k+.
- Delivery of a pilot or diagnostic at the target labor envelope.
- Expert reviewer feedback that the packet is useful, bounded, and not recklessly worded.
- At least 2 plausible referral partners among counsel, labs, consultants, or compliance advisors.

### No-Go Or Pause Criteria

Pause or reposition if:

- Buyers repeatedly say only law firms, labs, or certification bodies can solve the problem.
- Buyers value the work below $5k except in rare urgent cases.
- A standard packet requires more than 60 human hours at a sub-$15k price.
- Experts view the deliverable as creating unacceptable liability.
- Supplier documentation is consistently too unavailable to create a valuable buyer outcome.
- Customer acquisition depends on broad marketing rather than identifiable buyer triggers.

### Investment Committee View

Liberty Tree should treat the PFAS Evidence Packet as a high-potential wedge, not yet a proven company. The product is commercially credible only if sold as a practical evidence sprint for a specific business trigger. The first validation target is not $50k/month. The first target is proof that one buyer will pay a meaningful fixed fee, receive a premium packet, and say the output made a real business decision easier.

If that happens twice, the business deserves a more serious launch. If it does not, more templates, branding, or software will not fix the core problem.
